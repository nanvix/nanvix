### Issue 1: axiom_cache_lru_of_remove is sound but under-constrained in a subtle way — verify your mental model (Non-blocking)

**What you said:** The axiom is sound "because BTreeMap::remove doesn't change last_used counters of remaining entries."

**Why the axiom IS actually well-constrained (confirming your conclusion, with nuance):**

The axiom's precondition is:
```rust
btreemap_view_spec(new_entries) == btreemap_view_spec(old_entries).remove(key)
```

Because `btreemap_view_spec` maps `BTreeMap<K, CacheEntry<V>>` → `Map<K, CacheEntry<V>>` (not `Map<K, V>`), this constrains that all remaining entries have **identical `CacheEntry` values including `last_used` fields**. An adversarial `new_entries` with different `last_used` counters wouldn't satisfy the precondition. So the axiom can't be misapplied to unrelated BTreeMaps — it's well-constrained by the types.

However, there's a deeper question you should have raised in your Q3: **the axiom is currently only used for `remove` and `evict`, but `Cache::put` is `external_body`.** If you ever tried to body-verify `put`, you would need an analogous `axiom_cache_lru_of_insert` — and that axiom would be substantially harder to state correctly (you'd need to express that a fresh counter places the entry at the MRU end, which requires reasoning about counter monotonicity relative to all existing `last_used` values). Your analysis correctly notes this would increase trust, but you should explicitly document that the current architecture **fundamentally cannot support body-verifying `put`** without this second axiom — it's not just "not worth it," it's architecturally blocked.

**Severity:** Non-blocking. Your KEEP verdict is correct. But your Q3 answer ("making cache_lru_of fully interpreted") misses the real structural issue: the problem isn't that `cache_lru_of` is uninterpreted — it's that the *counter monotonicity invariant* linking concrete `last_used` values to abstract `lru_order` position is nowhere in the spec. Making `cache_lru_of` interpreted wouldn't help unless you also added this invariant, which would require reasoning about all entries' counters — exactly the infrastructure that makes body-verifying `get`/`put` hard.

### Issue 2: Counter overflow trust assumption is under-stated given external_body functions (Non-blocking, but raise severity)

**What's documented:** BUG-1 says counter overflow is LOW severity, physically unreachable.

**What's missed:** The soundness of `Cache::get` and `Cache::put` specs **depends on no overflow**. Both functions are `external_body` with specs claiming:
- `get`: `self@ == old(self)@.spec_get(*key).0` (entry moves to MRU position)  
- `put`: `self@ == old(self)@.spec_put(key, value)` (new entry at MRU position)

After counter wraparound, `self.counter += 1` produces a value **smaller** than existing `last_used` values. The implementation would then treat the newly-accessed entry as **older** than all others (it has the smallest counter), while the spec says it's MRU. This means:

1. The `external_body` spec on `get` becomes **unsound** — the implementation places the entry at LRU, but the spec says MRU.
2. Subsequent `evict` would evict the **most recently used** entry — the exact opposite of the spec.

This isn't just a theoretical concern — it means the `external_body` specs have an **implicit precondition** (`self.counter < u64::MAX`) that is not stated. In a verification context, unstated preconditions on trusted functions are the most dangerous kind of trust gap.

**Recommendation:** Either:
- Add `requires self.counter < u64::MAX` to `get` and `put` specs (makes the trust assumption explicit and verifier-checked), or
- Add a `saturating_add` / `checked_add` with a panic path, and document why the precondition is omitted.

The current state — unstated precondition on external_body functions — should be documented as a **MEDIUM** trust assumption, not LOW, because if it's violated the verification guarantees become **actively wrong** (not just missing).
