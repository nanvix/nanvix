Summary of the design:

**Abstract resource:** one x86 page-table entry = the pair `(flags, frame)`. Two composed Views mirroring the source structure.

- **`PteFlagsView`** — eight `bool`s: `present, writable, user, write_through, cache_disabled, accessed, dirty, cow`. Each two-valued enum projects to a `bool`; `closed view()` hides the `PteWord` bit-packing.
- **`PteView`** — `{ flags: PteFlagsView, frame: int }`, reusing the shipped `FrameNumber@` abstraction.

**Key PTE-specific decision:** `PageTableEntryFlags::new` takes **7** parameters (no cow); the spec pins `cow == false` (`NotCopyOnWrite`) as a default — load-bearing so `is_cow` on a fresh entry is determinate and stale-bit bugs are rejected. This is the main divergence from the sibling `pde` design (which has `large_page` instead).

**Invariants:** flags `inv` is vacuous (all 2⁸ combos legal — no `cow ⇒ present` coupling); entry `inv` carries only the `FrameNumber` bound.

**Spec transitions** cover all four in-scope functions: faithful flag/entry constructors, `is_present` as a pure projection, and entry-level `is_present` delegating to `self@.flags.present`.

Every field passes the substitution test (all are architecture-level concepts independent of bit layout), and the doc includes per-field rationale plus eight rejected alternatives (minimal flag views, omitting `cow`, flattening, storing raw words/addresses, open `view()`, invented invariants).
